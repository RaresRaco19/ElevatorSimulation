§2

architecture

Two layers, two questions

The handover between the layers is one committed stop — nothing richer. Any scheduler composes with any car policy, and a change to one cannot quietly alter the other.

§3

architecture

One tick, four steps

One tick = one floor. Boarding is instantaneous — which is why a car's arrival time is pure geometry, the property core/trip_estimator.py is built on.

§4

destination dispatch

The cost of a candidate car

Every term is an exact closed form from core/trip_estimator.py — candidate cars are scored with arithmetic, not with a simulated run.

§5

destination dispatch

Why the third term decides it

A nearest-car scheduler takes the closer car and drags its reversal point out. Only a scheduler that knows the destination up front can tell those two apart.

§5

destination dispatch

What the car's queue hides

Closed with a pledge ledger inside the scheduler rather than by changing the engine. Same-tick requests interact too, so a whole tick is placed by cheapest insertion — greedy, not globally optimal, and affordable because every evaluation is arithmetic.

§5

destination dispatch

The receipt

estimate drift is the gap between the wait a scheduler promised and the wait it delivered: 0.13 average against round_robin's 9.6. A computed arrival, not a distance guess.

§5

bounded detour

Bound the turn-back

Plain LOOK is unfair to whoever is standing behind a car that just went by — they wait out the whole remaining sweep plus the return leg. Turning back freely is worse: the car thrashes and everyone aboard pays, with no ceiling.

Worst case is exactly k · 2D = 16 ticks per sweep for everyone aboard — a guarantee, not a tendency. tests/test_car_policies.py checks it.

§6

bounded detour

One diversion, start to finish

A diversion is a sweep extension that snaps back instead of persisting — so the car resumes the trajectory it would have taken anyway.

§6

method

How I made it measurable

No public trace fits the input schema, so every scenario is hand-built to stress a named failure mode — not random traffic.

up-peak — single sourcedown-peak — many source lunch rush — reverse tripscapacity overload contradictory same-tick pairssingle-elevator bottleneck quiet / intermittent3 full-day composites — 10 / 35 / 50 floors

11 scenarios × 1–10 elevators × capacity 3/5/8/13 × 6 pairs ≈ 2640 runs in about 30 seconds

  • wait and total are charted separately — blending them hides the exact trade this project is about.
  • A config that doesn't converge leaves a gap in the trace. No fabricated points.

So nothing after this is one lucky run.

  • selfish is the worst weighting for both look and bounded_detour — the fairness term earns its place.
  • Margins are small: which scheduler you pick matters far more than how you weight it.
§8

measured, not argued

Two checks on the weights

  • W_WAIT is pinned at 1.0 in every preset: the cost only ranks cars and is never reported, so a common factor cancels. test_weights_reach_the_cost_function pins exactly that.
  • Under scan, balanced / fair / selfish score identically (17.2 / 39.9) across the whole fairness range — a consistency check, not a coincidence. A true-SCAN car reverses at the building's end regardless, so delta_imposed is permanently 0 and W_FAIRNESS has nothing to multiply.

§9

experiment design

Why the grid is a full 2 × 3

Not for completeness — so the two effects can be separated.

round_robin + look → round_robin + bounded_detour

What the detour policy is worth on its own, under a dispatcher that knows nothing.

19.0 → 18.3 wait

destination_dispatch + look → + bounded_detour

What the dispatcher gains specifically from knowing a car may detour: told the car's bounds, it stops pricing a pickup behind a car as a full reversal.

14.9 → 14.4 wait

The same swap, across every fleet size and capacity: the gap holds at every resourcing level, and at capacity 8 or more the default reaches with 4 cars what the baseline needs 10 to match.

§9

where it doesn't hold

Trade-offs, honestly

The detour policy is not universally better

On sample_full_day (4 cars, 35 floors) + look gets 4.3 avg wait / max 22; + bounded_detour gets 7.7 / max 34 — the reverse of the skyscraper result.

Both runs are committed to outputs/runs/ so the trade stays visible.

The default weights buy wait and pay in ride time

Strongest setting on skyscraper50 (+0.97) and stress (+0.51); third of five on full_day (−0.42) and downpeak40 (−0.99).

It won the sweep-wide mean — a mean, not a guarantee.

Fairness costs the mean and buys the tail

Both the D/k/F bounds and W_FAIRNESS make the average slightly worse in some scenarios and the worst case much better.

The fairness-vs-efficiency trade, chosen deliberately.

Capacity bites only at boarding

A full car passes a waiting passenger. Capacity pressure therefore shows up as wait, never as rejection.

Which keeps "serve every request eventually" true.

None of these turned up while writing slides — they are why the losing runs are committed to the repo.

§10

What's next

  1. Model door / boarding delay. The one change that costs trip_estimator its exactness — every closed form becomes an approximation and the scheduler's ETA stops being a promise. A deliberate next step, not an oversight.
  2. Wire in express. Implemented: cars opt in via serviceable_floors, both ends of a trip must be covered, nearest eligible car wins, and it fails fast rather than stranding a passenger. Not wired in because destination_dispatch treats the fleet as homogeneous — the real work is teaching the cost model eligibility, not exposing a flag.
  3. Adaptive weights. A scheduler that retuned to the traffic it is seeing would beat any single fixed choice. §10 is the evidence.

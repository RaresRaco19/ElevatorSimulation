Cost Weights

The three numbers that decide which car wins — src/elevator_sim/schedulers/destination_dispatch.py

Destination dispatch ranks every candidate car with one formula. These weights set what it cares about, and swapping them genuinely changes which car takes a request — so they are swept and charted rather than argued about.

The formula

Multipliers shown are the shipped defaults, chosen from the sweep below. They are fixed constants at the top of destination_dispatch.py; they stay constructor arguments only so the sweep can vary them, and the CLI does not expose them.

Presets

preset W_WAIT W_TRAVEL W_FAIRNESS what it expresses
default 1.00.250.25 The shipped setting, picked from the sweep: waiting outweighs everything else four to one.
ride-heavy 1.01.00.2 A tick riding is as bad as a tick waiting, and other passengers barely count.
balanced 1.00.50.5 What shipped before the sweep retuned the defaults — waiting hurts twice as much as riding.
fair 1.00.51.5 Delay imposed on others outweighs the new passenger's own wait.
selfish 1.00.50.0 Fairness term deleted — degenerates to nearest-car-with-a-real-ETA.

Declared in analysis/scripts/run_sweep.py. default is not hardcoded there — it is written (dd.W_WAIT, dd.W_TRAVEL, dd.W_FAIRNESS), so retuning the shipped constants moves this row automatically and it stays a true baseline instead of silently becoming a stale fourth variant.

Why W_WAIT is 1.0 everywhere

The same two cars as the worked example, scored at the shipped weights and at three times the shipped weights: the bars grow, the winner does not move. The cost ranks candidate cars and is never reported to anyone, so a common factor cancels out. Pinning W_WAIT at 1.0 in every preset therefore loses no generality and makes the other two columns read directly as “relative to waiting”. tests/test_schedulers.py's test_weights_reach_the_cost_function pins exactly that.

Average ticks over the whole sweep — 11 scenarios x 1–10 cars x capacities 3/5/8/13, 1320 runs per preset. Best in column marked, worst marked in red. default is the shipped setting, and this table is what chose it.

Three things to read off it. Turning fairness off costs you: selfish is the worst weighting for both look and bounded_detour, so the term earns its place. Under scan the weights barely register — balanced, fair and selfish score identically (17.2 / 39.9) despite spanning the whole fairness range. That is a consistency check rather than a coincidence: a true-SCAN car reverses at the building's end no matter what, so delta_imposed is permanently 0 and W_FAIRNESS has nothing to multiply. And the margins are small — which scheduler you pick matters far more than how you weight it.

The average hides a real trade. default wins the sweep-wide mean but is not uniformly better. Per scenario, with bounded_detour, it is the strongest setting on sample_full_day_skyscraper50 (+0.97 ticks on the next best) and sample_stress (+0.51), but only third of five on sample_full_day (−0.42) and sample_downpeak_highrise40 (−0.99). Weighting ride time low buys a shorter wait and pays for it in time aboard. outputs/runs/ keeps a curated run of each kind committed so both sides stay visible.

Running it yourself

python analysis/scripts/run_sweep.py --weights
python analysis/scripts/compare_schedulers.py --weights

The first writes outputs/weights/manifest.json (~6600 runs, about half a minute, stats only — no per-run folders). The second charts it as analysis/figures/weight_comparison_wait_time.png and _total_time.png, grouped by preset with one bar per car policy.

To try a weighting other than the shipped one, edit the three constants in destination_dispatch.py and re-run. They are deliberately not CLI flags: they describe how the scheduler behaves rather than what one run did, the same reasoning that keeps bounded_detour's D/k/F bounds out of the CLI. Whichever values are in force are recorded in every run's config.json.

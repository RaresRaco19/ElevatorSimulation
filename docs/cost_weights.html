Cost Weights

The three numbers that decide which car wins — src/elevator_sim/schedulers/destination_dispatch.py

Destination dispatch ranks every candidate car with one formula. These weights set what it cares about, and swapping them genuinely changes which car takes a request — so they are swept and charted rather than argued about.

The formula

Multipliers shown are the shipped defaults. They are module constants at the top of destination_dispatch.py and constructor arguments on the scheduler, so any run can be scored under any weighting without editing the file.

Presets

preset W_WAIT W_TRAVEL W_FAIRNESS what it expresses
default 1.00.50.5 The shipped setting: waiting hurts twice as much as riding.
ride-heavy 1.01.00.2 A tick riding is as bad as a tick waiting, and other passengers barely count.
wait-first 1.00.250.25 Waiting dominates everything — minimise it at the cost of longer rides.
fair 1.00.51.5 Delay imposed on others outweighs the new passenger's own wait.
selfish 1.00.50.0 Fairness term deleted — degenerates to nearest-car-with-a-real-ETA.

Declared in analysis/scripts/run_sweep.py. default is not hardcoded there — it is written (dd.W_WAIT, dd.W_TRAVEL, dd.W_FAIRNESS), so retuning the shipped constants moves this row automatically and it stays a true baseline instead of silently becoming a stale fourth variant.

Why W_WAIT is 1.0 everywhere

The cost ranks candidate cars and is never reported to anyone, so a common factor cancels out. Pinning W_WAIT at 1.0 in every preset therefore loses no generality and makes the other two columns read directly as “relative to waiting”. tests/test_schedulers.py's test_weights_reach_the_cost_function pins exactly that.

Average ticks over the whole sweep — 11 scenarios x 1–10 cars x capacities 3/5/8/13, 1320 runs per preset. Best in column marked, worst marked in red.

Two things to read off it. Turning fairness off costs you: selfish is the worst weighting for both look and bounded_detour, so the term earns its place. And under scan the weights barely register — fair and selfish score identically (17.2 / 39.9) despite sitting at opposite ends of the fairness range. That is a consistency check rather than a coincidence: a true-SCAN car reverses at the building's end no matter what, so delta_imposed is permanently 0 and W_FAIRNESS has nothing to multiply.

Running it yourself

python analysis/scripts/run_sweep.py --weights
python analysis/scripts/compare_schedulers.py --weights

The first writes outputs/weights/manifest.json (~6600 runs, about half a minute, stats only — no per-run folders). The second charts it as analysis/figures/weight_comparison_wait_time.png and _total_time.png, grouped by preset with one bar per car policy.

python -m elevator_sim.run_simulation --scheduler destination_dispatch --car-policy look \
    --requests data/requests/sample_full_day.csv --elevators 4 --floors 35 --capacity 8 \
    --w-travel 1.0 --w-fairness 0.2 --out outputs/dd_ride_heavy_full_day

To watch one weighting instead of averaging it: the --w-* flags produce an ordinary run folder that shows up in the ui/ playback app, and the effective weights are recorded in its config.json. Omitted flags fall back to the module constants, so every command that doesn't mention them is unaffected.

See docs/destination_dispatch.html for what the three terms mean and a worked example of the fairness term deciding a request, src/elevator_sim/schedulers/destination_dispatch.py for the implementation, and analysis/README.md for the sweep.

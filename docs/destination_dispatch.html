Destination Dispatch Scheduler

Picks the cheapest car, not the nearest one — src/elevator_sim/schedulers/destination_dispatch.py

Every other scheduler here answers “which car is closest to the pickup?” — a question about the first leg only. This one knows where the passenger is going, so it can also ask what the trip costs the people already promised that car.

The cost

Fixed constants at the top of destination_dispatch.py, chosen from the sweep rather than by judgement. Waiting is weighted heaviest because standing at a floor with no information is worse than riding in a car you can see moving. See cost_weights.html for how these were settled and what they trade away.

Setting the weights

Three rules of thumb. Only the ratios matter — the cost is used to rank cars, never reported, so doubling all three changes nothing. W_FAIRNESS is the one that changes behaviour: at 0 the scheduler degenerates to nearest-car-with-a-real-ETA, and raising it trades a longer wait for the new passenger against protecting people already promised a car. W_TRAVEL is the least useful knob, because ride time is mostly fixed by the building's geometry rather than by which car you pick — it is kept below W_WAIT so a short ride can never outvote a long wait.

These values are judgement, not a fit — so there is a sweep for settling it. run_sweep.py --weights scores five named presets against all three car policies over every scenario at 1–10 cars and four capacities, and compare_schedulers.py --weights charts them, so a weight that only helps one traffic pattern shows up as a wash. The shipped values came out of that sweep; they are fixed constants rather than CLI flags, because they describe how the scheduler behaves rather than what one run did. The exact presets, what each one scores, and what the shipped setting trades away are in cost_weights.html.

What that sweep says today: the weights move far less than the scheduler choice does. Turning fairness off is the clearest loss (selfish costs look about a tick of avg total time), and under scan the weights barely register at all — a car that always reverses at the building's end imposes nothing on anyone, so delta_imposed is permanently 0 and W_FAIRNESS has nothing to multiply.

Why that third term decides it

A nearest-car scheduler takes E2 — it is one floor from the pickup, E1 is two. But E2 only runs as far as 6, so carrying this passenger to 9 drags its reversal point out three floors, and the passenger at floor 1 pays for every one of them twice.

What the car's queue hides

core/engine.py adds a destination to stop_queue only at boarding, so a lobby car with eight people assigned and none aboard reads as completely idle — and an up-peak burst would pile onto it. Requests arriving on the same tick interact the same way, so the engine hands over the whole tick at once and they are placed by cheapest insertion, re-scoring after every commitment.

It has to know how cars turn

Where a car reverses is the car policy's rule, not the scheduler's — so core/engine.py hands the active policy over via Scheduler.bind_car_policy() and trip_estimator takes the turn point as a parameter. The same channel tells the dispatcher when a car may turn back for a pickup behind it; without it, every such pickup would be priced at the cost of a full reversal. See docs/bounded_detour.html.

Measured

Ticks on the project's biggest scenario, same requests and same cars — only the scheduler differs. estimate drift is the gap between the wait a scheduler promised and the one it delivered: round_robin guesses floor distance, this one reports the arrival it computed.
